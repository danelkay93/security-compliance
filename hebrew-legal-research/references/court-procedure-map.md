# Court and Procedure Map (navigation only)

Use this when a user asks "which court", "how do I file", "can I appeal", or brings a dispute such as construction defects. It is a map for routing the user to the right statute and source, not advice on what to do in their case. Every rule below cites its section; re-check amounts and deadlines at the source before quoting them, because they change.

## 1. Which court hears it

| Forum | What it hears | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Small Claims Court (בית משפט לתביעות קטנות) | Civil claims by an INDIVIDUAL up to an index-linked ceiling (39,900 NIS in 2026): money, or an order to replace or repair goods or cancel a transaction worth up to the same ceiling. The court may transfer a filer who brought more than five claims that year in the same court. No lawyer unless the court allows it. | Courts Law s.60(a)-(b), s.63(a) |
| Magistrate Court (בית משפט השלום) | Civil claims (other than land claims, which have their own rules) up to 2,500,000 NIS, plus less serious criminal cases | Courts Law s.51(a) |
| District Court (בית המשפט המחוזי) | Anything not within another court's jurisdiction; appeals from Magistrate Courts | Courts Law s.40, s.52 |
| Administrative Court (the District Court sitting as בית משפט לעניינים מינהליים) | Petitions against decisions of authorities on matters listed in the First Schedule, plus the appeals and claims in the Second and Third Schedules | Administrative Courts Law 2000 s.5 |
| Superintendent of Land Registration (המפקח על רישום מקרקעין) | Disputes between apartment owners in a condominium about rights and duties under the by-laws and the listed sections (outside the court hierarchy) | Land Law 1969 s.72 |
| Family Court (בית המשפט לענייני משפחה) | Family matters. It is a Magistrate Court designated by order to sit as a Family Court | Family Courts Law 1995 s.2(a) |
| Labor Courts (בית הדין לעבודה) | Employment disputes and National Insurance (Bituach Leumi) claims; regional and national levels | Labor Courts Law 1969 |
| High Court of Justice (בג"ץ) | Relief "for the sake of justice" in matters outside any other court's jurisdiction, chiefly petitions against public authorities NOT covered by the Administrative Court | Basic Law: The Judiciary s.15(c)-(d) |

**Which city (local venue).** Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 reg. 7(a): a claim is filed in the court of the judicial district where the defendant lives or does business, or where the act or omission occurred; a claim over land goes to the district where the land is; and if the parties agreed on a venue, the claim is filed in the agreed court. So check the sale or contractor contract for a forum clause. Jurisdiction objections should be raised at the first opportunity; check the Regulations for the current rule before stating the timing.

## 2. Appeal map

| From | To | As of right or by leave | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magistrate Court judgment | District Court | As of right | Courts Law s.52(a) |
| Magistrate Court interim decision (civil) | District Court | Leave only | Courts Law s.52(b) |
| Small Claims judgment | District Court (single judge) | Leave only | Courts Law s.64 |
| District Court first-instance judgment | Supreme Court | As of right | Courts Law s.41(a) |
| District Court interim decision, or a District ruling given on appeal | Supreme Court | Leave only (רע"א) | Courts Law s.41(b) |

Deadlines are set by regulations and do change. Example: Kol Zchut reports that for small-claims proceedings opened from 11.04.2025 the leave-to-appeal application is due within 30 days (15 days for earlier proceedings). Always confirm the deadline for the user's proceeding.

## 3. Precedent: what binds

- A Supreme Court holding (הלכה פסוקה) binds every court except the Supreme Court itself; a holding of any other court only GUIDES the courts below it (Basic Law: The Judiciary s.20).
- Before calling a holding "controlling": confirm it was the holding and not obiter, check for a later further hearing (דיון נוסף) or a later Supreme Court ruling that departed from it, and give the official reporter citation (פ"ד volume(part) page) only if a database shows it.
- If no database is reachable, say plainly that you cannot confirm the holding is still controlling.

## 4. Filing and costs

- Small claims can be filed digitally through the Judicial Authority site or on paper at the court (Kol Zchut, "הגשת תביעה קטנה").
- Court fees: gov.il fees page (https://www.gov.il/he/pages/fees_16). Do not compute a fee from memory.
- Legal aid: the Legal Aid Law 1972 and the Ministry of Justice legal-aid department. Eligibility is set by the law and its regulations, so route the user there rather than stating eligibility.
- After judgment, enforcement runs through the Execution Office (הוצאה לפועל) under the Execution Law 1967.

## 5. Worked routing: construction defects ("ליקויי בנייה")

Ask first: did the user BUY the apartment from the developer, or HIRE a contractor (renovation, self-build)?

**Bought a new apartment from the developer: Sale (Apartments) Law 1973.**
1. Inspection period (tkufat bedek): starts at handover; its length depends on the defect type (1 to 7 years per Kol Zchut, listed in the law's Schedule). During it the seller is liable unless it proves the buyer caused the defect (s.4(a)(2)).
2. Warranty period (tkufat achrayut): three years after the inspection period ends; the buyer must prove the defect stems from design, work or materials (s.4(a)(3), s.4(c)).
3. Notice (s.4A): a defect discoverable at handover must be notified to the seller within one year of handover; a hidden defect within a reasonable time after discovery. Ask whether and when notice was given.
4. Repair opportunity (s.4B): the buyer must give the seller a proper opportunity to repair.
5. Which version: if the building was completed, or the sale contract signed, before 06.04.2011, the earlier version of the law and its inspection-period table apply (s.11(c)). Check both dates.
6. Common property: defects in the building's common parts raise the question of who sues and, between owners, the condominium track (section 1). Ask whether the defect is inside the apartment or in common property.
7. Evidence: defect claims are usually proved by an engineer's expert opinion, which a party may attach to its pleading (Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 reg. 15(b)); the court may also appoint its own expert. Route the choice of expert to an advocate.
8. Time to sue: the inspection and warranty periods are LIABILITY periods, not filing deadlines. The claim itself is subject to the Limitation Law (s.5(1) seven years, s.6 start, s.8 late discovery, s.15A Swords of Iron). When the clock starts is a case-law question; do not compute it.
9. Other routes: a complaint to the Registrar of Contractors if the developer does not repair; a claim in court (Small Claims, Magistrate or District by amount, see section 1), in the venue under reg. 7(a).

**Hired a contractor: Contractor Contract Law 1974.**
- s.3: notify the contractor of a defect within a reasonable time after discovering it (or when it should have been discovered) and, if repairable, give a proper opportunity to repair. Without notice the client cannot rely on the defect unless the contractor knew of it.

Then hand the user to an advocate before filing: whether a defect qualifies, its period, and the claim value are case-specific questions.
