# Key Israeli Legislation Index

## Overview
This index covers the most commonly referenced Israeli laws, organized by area.
For each law, the Hebrew name, year of enactment, and key sections are provided.

## Constitutional / Basic Laws (Chukei Yesod)

### Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty (1992)
- Hebrew: Chok Yesod: Kvod HaAdam VeCheruto
- Key sections: Saif 2 (protection of life, body, dignity), Saif 7 (privacy rights)
- Significance: Quasi-constitutional status, basis for judicial review
- Amended: 1994 (added the "Basic principles" clause; the original purpose clause was re-designated). The limitation clause (Saif 8) existed in the 1992 enactment and was amended in 1994.
- Limitation clause (Saif 8): a right may be infringed only by a law befitting the values of the State of Israel, for a proper purpose, and to an extent no greater than required. So a regular law CAN infringe a Basic-Law right if it meets this test; "a Basic Law cannot be overridden by regular legislation" is wrong as a general statement.

### Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation (1994, re-enacted; orig. 1992)
- Hebrew: Chok Yesod: Chofesh HaIsuk
- Key sections: Saif 3 (right to engage in any occupation), Saif 7 (entrenchment: changed only by a Basic Law passed by a majority of Knesset members), Saif 8 (override: a law passed by such a majority that says so expressly is valid despite the Basic Law, and lapses after four years unless it sets an earlier date)
- Significance: Economic freedom protection; the only Basic Law with an express override clause covered here

### Basic Law: The Knesset (1958)
- Hebrew: Chok Yesod: HaKnesset
- Key sections: Election law, Knesset immunity, basic parliamentary rules

### Basic Law: The Government (2001)
- Hebrew: Chok Yesod: HaMemshala
- Key sections: Formation of government, PM powers, ministerial responsibility

### Basic Law: The Judiciary (1984)
- Hebrew: Chok Yesod: HaShfita
- Key sections: Section 15 (Supreme Court, including its jurisdiction sitting as the High Court of Justice / Bagatz), Section 20 (binding precedent: Supreme Court rulings bind lower courts but not the Supreme Court itself)
- Significance: Constitutional anchor for judicial review and HCJ authority; central to the 2023-2026 judicial-reform track below

### Judicial review of ordinary statutes (the "constitutional revolution")
- Rests on CA 6821/93 United Mizrahi Bank v. Migdal Cooperative Village (ע"א 6821/93 בנק המזרחי המאוחד בע"מ נ' מגדל כפר שיתופי), decided 9.11.1995: Basic Laws on rights have constitutional status and an ordinary law that infringes them must meet the limitation clause.
- It predates the free Supreme Court search (rulings from 1997), so take the full reporter citation from Nevo; do not reconstruct it. The 2024 ruling on review of Basic Laws themselves is a separate, later step.

## Contract Law

### Contracts Law (General Part) 1973
- Hebrew: Chok HaChozim (Chelek Klali)
- Key sections:
  - Saif 1: Contract formation (offer and acceptance)
  - Saif 12: Good faith in negotiations (tom lev)
  - Saif 14: Mistake (ta'ut); Saif 15: Misrepresentation (hata'aya, including non-disclosure)
  - Saif 17: Duress (kfiya); Saif 18: Exploitation (oshek: exploiting distress, weakness or inexperience, with terms unreasonably worse than usual); Saif 19: Partial cancellation

  - Saif 25: Contract interpretation (two versions since Amendment 3, see the Judicial Reform Track section below)
  - Saif 39: Good faith in performance
- Note: No consideration requirement (unlike common law systems)

## Employment Law

### Employment Law Overview
Israeli employment law is heavily protective of employees and consists of
multiple separate statutes:

### Severance Pay Law 1963
- Hebrew: Chok Pitzuei Piturin
- Key sections:
  - Saif 1: Right to severance after 1 year of continuous employment
  - Saif 12(a): Rate. One month's salary per year for a salaried (monthly) employee; the statute itself says two weeks per year for a wage (hourly/daily) employee, and s.12(b) lets the minister raise that rate by regulation. Regulations have been issued under s.12(b), so check the current rate for wage employees before stating it.
  - Saif 14: Section 14 arrangement (pension-fund payments replace severance where an agreement or approval provides so)

### Hours of Work and Rest Law 1951
- Hebrew: Chok Shaot Avoda UMenucha
- Key sections: Maximum work hours, overtime, rest day (yom menucha)
- Statute (Saif 3): a work week may not exceed 45 hours. The 42-hour standard week comes from the 2018 extension order on shortening the work week (in force 01.04.2018), not from the statute itself; cite each to its source.

### Annual Leave Law 1951
- Hebrew: Chok Chufsha Shnatit
- Key sections: Minimum vacation days by seniority

### Sick Pay Law 1976
- Hebrew: Chok Dmei Machala
- Key sections: Sick leave entitlement, medical certificate requirements

### Minimum Wage Law 1987
- Hebrew: Chok Schar Minimum
- Updated periodically by government order

### Equal Employment Opportunity Law 1988
- Hebrew: Chok Shivion Hizdamniut BaAvoda
- Key sections: Prohibited discrimination grounds, burden of proof

### Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law 1998
- Hebrew: Chok Meni'at Hatrada Minit
- Key sections: Definition, employer obligations, complaint procedures

### Protection of Wages Law 1958
- Hebrew: Chok Haganat HaSachar
- Pay dates, permitted deductions, and late-wage compensation (pitzui halanat sachar, s.17). Do not compute the compensation; route to a calculator or professional.

### Mandatory pension (extension order)
- The comprehensive-pension extension order published 01.01.2008 made pension contributions mandatory for employees across the economy, phased in to 2013, with separate employer contributions toward severance. Check the current rates on Kol Zchut rather than quoting them.

### Notice of Dismissal and Resignation Law 2001
- Hebrew: Chok Hoda'a Mukdemet LePiturin ULeHitpitarut

## Real Estate Law

### Land Law 1969
- Hebrew: Chok HaMikrkain
- Key sections: Ownership (baalut), leasehold, easements (zikat hanaa, Saif 5; a right of way is one kind)
- Land registration: Tabu (Land Registry). Saif 125: registration is conclusive evidence for settled land, prima facie evidence for unsettled land
- Saif 159(b): the Limitation Law does not apply to claims to realize a right in settled land (mekarkein musdarim)
- Chapter 6: condominiums (batim meshutafim): apartments and common property, by-laws, management, changes to common property and building rights, and dispute resolution. A dispute between apartment owners about their rights or duties under the by-laws or the listed sections is decided by the Superintendent of Land Registration (המפקח על רישום מקרקעין), not a court (s.72).

### Planning and Building Law 1965
- Hebrew: Chok HaTichnun VeHaBniya
- Key sections: Building permits, zoning, planning committees

### Sale (Apartments) Law 1973
- Hebrew: Chok HaMecher (Dirot), 5733-1973
- Governs DEFECTS in a new apartment sold by the developer-seller (a "seller" is one who sells an apartment it built or will build, including the subsequent buyer's claims, "kone mishne")
- Saif 4: non-conformity (i-hat'ama). During the inspection period (tkufat bedek, length per defect type in the Schedule, starting at handover) the seller is liable unless it proves the buyer caused the defect; during the following three-year warranty period (tkufat achrayut) the buyer must prove the defect stems from design, work or materials; a separate 20-year rule covers a fundamental (structural) defect
- Saif 4A: NOTICE. A defect that was discoverable at handover can be relied on only if the buyer notified the seller within one year of handover; a defect not discoverable on reasonable inspection, within a reasonable time after discovering it. A missed notice can sink the claim, so ask about it first.
- Saif 4B: the buyer must give the seller a proper opportunity to repair before claiming repair costs
- Two versions: Amendment 5 (in force 6.4.2011) does not apply to an apartment in a building whose construction ended, or whose sale contract was made, before 6.4.2011; such apartments keep the earlier version and its inspection-period table (s.11(c)). Check both the completion date and the contract date before quoting any period.
- These are LIABILITY periods, not filing deadlines: the claim itself is subject to the Limitation Law (s.5(1) seven years, s.6 start, s.8 late discovery). When the clock starts in a defects case is a case-law question; route it to an advocate.
- If the developer does not repair, a complaint can also be filed with the Registrar of Contractors (Rasham HaKablanim) at the Ministry of Construction and Housing

### Contractor Contract Law 1974
- Hebrew: Chok Chozeh Kablanut, 5734-1974
- Applies when the owner HIRED a contractor (renovation, self-build), not when buying from a developer. Ask which situation applies.
- Saif 3: the client must notify the contractor of a defect within a reasonable time after discovering it (or when it should have been discovered) and, if repairable, give a proper opportunity to repair; without notice the client cannot rely on the defect unless the contractor knew of it

### Sale (Apartments) (Assurance of Investments) Law 1974
- Hebrew: Chok Mecher Dirot (Havtachat Hashka'ot)
- Covers only the SECURITY of the buyer's payments (bank guarantee, insurance), not defects

### Tenant Protection Law 1972
- Hebrew: Chok Haganat HaDayar
- Key sections: Protected tenancy, rent control (limited application today)

### Rent and Borrowing Law 1971
- Hebrew: Chok HaSechirut VeHaShee'la

## Privacy and Data Protection

### Privacy Protection Law 1981
- Hebrew: Chok Haganat HaPratiut
- Last major reform: **Amendment 13, in force August 14, 2025**, granting the Privacy Protection Authority independent supervision and administrative-fine powers and an expanded DPO regime.
- Breach notification: a severe security incident must be reported to the PPA **immediately** under the Privacy Protection (Data Security) Regulations; the PPA fines late reporting (see the Menora notice). It is not the GDPR 72-hour wording.
- Sanctions: significant administrative financial penalties (עיצום כספי). For the tariff and how it scales, consult the PPA's Amendment-13 professional guide rather than quoting a number from memory. Reductions apply in practice (see the Menora example below).
- Statutory damages without proof of harm depend on the head cited: **Section 29A allows up to NIS 50,000 without proof of harm, and up to double that (NIS 100,000) only where it is proven the infringement was committed with intent to harm**; these Section 29A amounts are index-linked (base: May 2007), so the current figure is higher. Amendment 13 added Section 15A, exemplary damages (פיצויים לדוגמה) not dependent on harm, capped at **NIS 10,000** for listed database breaches. Do not cite "NIS 100,000 without proof of harm" unconditionally.
- Enforcement: the PPA publishes its Amendment-13 fines as notices on its gov.il site (https://www.gov.il/he/departments/the_privacy_protection_authority). Example from that site: **NIS 256,000 on Menora Mivtachim Insurance (22/10/26)** for reporting a severe security incident late; the base amount was NIS 640,000 and was cut 60% under the reduction grounds in the law's Fifth Schedule. Cite enforcement examples only from the PPA's own notices, not from second-hand summaries, and check the site for newer ones.
- See detailed coverage in israeli-privacy-shield skill.

## Courts, Civil Procedure and Limitations

### Courts Law [Consolidated Version] 1984
- Hebrew: Chok Batei HaMishpat (Nusach Meshulav)
- Court hierarchy: Magistrate (Shalom), District (Mechozi), Supreme (Elyon); appeal as of right (ערעור בזכות) vs. leave-to-appeal (בקשת רשות ערעור / רע"א)
- Small Claims (תביעות קטנות): Sections 59-65. Jurisdiction (Section 60): a civil claim filed by an INDIVIDUAL (not a company); the court may transfer a filer who already brought more than five claims that year in the same court. Representation by a lawyer is allowed ONLY with the court's permission and for special recorded reasons (Section 63(a)). Appeal only with leave of a District Court judge (Section 64). For proceedings opened from 11.04.2025 the leave application is due within 30 days (15 days for earlier proceedings), per Kol Zchut. Claims can be filed digitally through the Judicial Authority site.
- Ceiling: the small-claims monetary ceiling is index-linked (Section 60 states 30,000 NIS adjusted to August 2008). It is 39,900 NIS in 2026. Treat any figure as time-sensitive and re-verify on Kol Zchut or the courts portal before quoting.
- Magistrate Court civil ceiling: 2,500,000 NIS for claims other than land claims (Section 51(a)(2)); the District Court hears what is not within another court's jurisdiction (Section 40).
- Appeal map: a Magistrate Court judgment is appealable to the District Court as of right (Section 52(a)); a Magistrate interim decision only by leave (Section 52(b)); a District Court first-instance judgment is appealable to the Supreme Court as of right (Section 41(a)); a District Court ruling given on appeal only by leave (Section 41(b), רע"א). See references/court-procedure-map.md.

### Limitation Law 1958
- Hebrew: Chok HaHityashnut
- Section 5: 7 years for non-land claims (Section 5(1)); for land, 15 years, or 25 years where the land was registered in the land books after settlement of title under the Land (Settlement of Title) Ordinance (Section 5(2))
- Interaction: Land Law 1969 Section 159(b) says the Limitation Law does not apply to claims to realize a right in SETTLED land (mekarkein musdarim). So for settled land there is generally no limitation period on such claims; do not quote "25 years for registered land" as the rule.
- Section 6: the period starts on the day the cause of action arose; Section 8: if the facts were unknown to the plaintiff for reasons beyond their control, it starts when they learned them.
- Swords of Iron suspension (Amendment 8, 2024, Section 15A): (a) a NON-land claim whose period had not ended by 25.1.2024: the period from 7 October 2023 to 6 April 2024 is not counted; (b) a LAND claim only if its period would have ended between 25.1.2024 and 6 April 2024; (c) periods that ended between 7 October 2023 and 24.1.2024 are treated as not yet ended, under the section's conditions. Read s.15A before applying it.

### Civil Procedure Regulations 5779-2018
- Hebrew: Takanot Seder HaDin HaEzrachi
- Entered into force 1 January 2021, replacing the 1984 regulations
- Opening principles: procedural good faith (tom lev) and proportionality (no disproportionate or abusive use of process). These framing principles govern how all civil proceedings are read.

## Judicial Reform Track (2024-2026)

This is not a single statute but a connected legislative and doctrinal track that any 2026 Israeli legal research touches. Treat the items below as a status snapshot as of 2026-11-03, not as background; details change week to week. Items marked "press reports" rest on news coverage, not on a court or Knesset document; check current status at https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/ and the Knesset legislation database before relying on any of them.

### Reasonableness clause (struck down)
- HCJ judgment of **2024-01-01**, two distinct holdings in the same case:
  - **8-7 majority** struck down the July 2023 amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary that barred courts from applying extreme-unreasonableness review to elected officials. Doctrine of extreme unreasonableness restored.
  - **Large majority (reported as 12 of 15)** held that HCJ has authority to strike down Basic Laws in exceptional and extreme circumstances. This is a separate, more foundational ruling.
- Citation hygiene: do not cite "8-7" for the Basic-Laws-review authority point.

### Judicial Selection Committee reform (passed, takes effect with the new Knesset)
- Basic Law amendment passed in **March 2025** (67-1).
- Activation: takes effect **when the next Knesset begins its term**. Elections for the 26th Knesset were set for **2026-10-27**; Basic Law: The Knesset s.12 requires the first sitting within 14 days of election day. Until the new Knesset convenes the current committee composition applies; do not state that it has convened without checking.
- Press reports: an **11-justice HCJ panel** heard the consolidated petitions in June 2026 and issued conditional orders (order nisi, צו על תנאי); after the Knesset's supplementary arguments in July 2026 the case returned to the panel for a ruling. **No final ruling found as of 2026-11-03**; check before stating which composition governs.
- Separate case (press reports): on **2026-05-31** a unanimous HCJ panel held that the Justice Minister is obligated to convene the CURRENT committee as soon as possible to fill District Court vacancies.

### 2026 "Judicial Overhaul Redux" measures (DIFFERENT stages, do not blanket-label as pending)
Each item is at its own stage. Track the current status before citing; do not present a mid-process bill as law, or an enacted measure as fully operative.
- **Mahash (Police Investigation Department), ENACTED but partly FROZEN (press reports).** Passed its final reading on **2026-06-11** (43-39). Reported on **2026-09-04**: an HCJ interim order (2-1) freezing the provisions on the department's new leadership structure, appointments and separate budget; the hearing on the petitions is set for **2026-12-28**. Present it as an enacted law whose key provisions are suspended pending the hearing.
- **AG split bill, first reading only (press reports).** Passed first reading (65-47) in June 2026 and was not enacted before the 25th Knesset ended. It continues in the 26th Knesset only if the continuity rule (דין רציפות) is applied, when a government formed in the new Knesset (or, for a committee or private bill, the committee) announces in the plenum its wish to apply it, and for an MK's bill only if that MK sits in the new Knesset and consents (Continuity of Bills Law 1993, ss.1-3); it is eligible because it passed first reading.
- **Ministerial legal advisers bill, preliminary reading only (press reports).** Passed a preliminary reading (58-50) on **2026-01-07**. Continuity applies only to bills that passed first reading in the previous Knesset, so this bill does not carry over; it would have to be re-introduced.
- **Civil Service Commissioner** (press reports): the live development is a ruling, not a bill. Reported on 2026-02-04: the HCJ held that the government is NOT required to appoint the commissioner through a competitive process, reversing an earlier decision.

### Doctrinal shifts to flag
- **Amendment 3 to the Contracts Law (General Part), Section 25(a), third reading 2026-01-05**. The statute now carries TWO versions of s.25(a): one for contracts made up to 6.1.2026 (the prior Apropim-era rule: intent as it appears from the contract and the circumstances) and one for contracts made or renewed from 7.1.2026. Under the new version: (1) the parties' own agreement on how to interpret the contract governs; (2) a BUSINESS contract (חוזה עסקי) with no such clause is interpreted by its language alone, unless that yields an unreasonable result or an internal contradiction; (4) a non-business contract, a standard-form contract (even if it says otherwise), an employment contract and a collective agreement are interpreted by the parties' intent; (5) parties not represented by a lawyer in drafting are treated as having NOT agreed on an interpretation method. It is a statutory amendment, not a case-law reversal. Always check the contract date first.
