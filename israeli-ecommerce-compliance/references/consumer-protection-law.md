# Israeli Consumer Protection Law, E-Commerce Requirements

## Overview
The Consumer Protection Law, 5741-1981 (Chok Hagnat HaTzarchan) is Israel's primary consumer
protection legislation. Section 14ג and the sections after it govern distance selling (mecher
merachok). Section numbers below refer to that law unless stated otherwise.

## Distance Selling Rules (Mecher Merachok)

### Definition
A distance transaction ("עסקת מכר מרחוק", s.14ג(ו)) is a sale of goods or services made following
distance marketing, without the parties being present together. Online stores, social-media shops
with payment links, phone sales and TV shopping all qualify. The rules protect a "צרכן" (buying
mainly for personal, household or family use) dealing with a "עוסק" (selling in the course of
business). B2B sales are outside them.

### Required Pre-Purchase Disclosures (s.14ג(א))
1. Name, ID or company number, and address of the business in Israel and abroad
2. Main characteristics of the product or service
3. Price and possible payment terms
4. Date and manner of supply
5. The period the offer is valid
6. Warranty details
7. The cancellation right

### Written Disclosure Document (s.14ג(ב))
Delivered in writing, in Hebrew or the language of the marketing, no later than the time of supply.
Contents: the identity and characteristics above, the price and payment terms that apply, how to
cancel, the manufacturer's name and country of manufacture, warranty information, and any other
terms of the deal.

## Cooling-Off Periods

### Standard Period (s.14ג(ג))
- Goods: from the day of the transaction until 14 days after receiving the goods or the disclosure document, whichever is later
- Services: within 14 days of the transaction or the disclosure document, whichever is later. A continuing service can be cancelled even after it started; a one-off service only at least 2 days (not counting rest days) before it is due
- The notice may be given by any channel the business must accept (see Cancellation Channels). It does not have to be in writing

### Extended Period (4 Months, s.14ג1(ג))
Applies to a person with a disability, a senior citizen (65+) and a new immigrant (within 5 years of
the oleh certificate), ONLY where the transaction included a conversation between the business and
the consumer, including a conversation over electronic communication. A purely self-service web or
app checkout gets the standard period. The business may ask for the certificate proving eligibility,
but no further proof.

## Cancellation Fee and Returns (s.14ה)
- Change of mind: a fee of up to 5% of the price or 100 NIS, whichever is **lower**
- That "fee" already includes shipping, packaging and any other cost the business says it incurred (s.14ה(ד)), so return or outbound shipping cannot be charged on top
- The consumer returns the goods to the business's place of business (the return trip is the consumer's, but nothing beyond the cap may be charged)
- Defect, non-conformity with the disclosed details, late supply or other breach by the business: no fee at all, and the consumer makes the goods available where they were delivered and notifies the business
- Continuing service already started: the proportional price of the service given
- Home installation: up to 100 NIS
- Significant deterioration of the goods: the business may claim damages (s.14ה(ג)); it may not impose a deduction or demand original packaging as a condition

## Refund Timeline
- The business returns the amount paid within 14 days of receiving the cancellation notice, cancels the charge, and gives the consumer a copy of the charge-cancellation notice
- Failing to refund after a distance-sale cancellation, or billing a subscription after a cancellation notice, can lead to exemplary damages of up to 10,000 NIS without proof of harm, and up to 50,000 NIS for a repeat breach (within two years of an earlier breach of the same provision that led to a conviction), a breach continued after an Authority notice or court order, or aggravated circumstances (s.31א(ג))
- A credit note instead of money is lawful only where the consumer is not entitled to a refund (s.14ז), and must then carry the full amount paid, be valid for at least 2 years, not depend on showing the invoice, and carry no restriction

## Exceptions to the Cancellation Right (s.14ג(ד))
These five are the only statutory exceptions for distance sales:
1. Perishable goods
2. Accommodation, travel, holiday or leisure services, if the cancellation falls within 7 days (not counting rest days) before the service date
3. "Information" as defined in the Computers Law (digital content, software)
4. Goods made specially for the consumer following the transaction
5. Goods that can be recorded, reproduced or copied, whose original packaging the consumer opened

The Consumer Protection (Cancellation of Transaction) Regulations 2010, reg. 6, contain a longer
list (underwear and swimwear, food products, medicines and supplements,
furniture assembled at home, and others). That list limits only the cancellation right under those
regulations (cancellations under s.14ו) and does NOT limit a distance-sale cancellation under s.14ג.

## Cancellation Channels (s.14ט)
- The business must accept notice by phone or orally, registered mail, email, fax (if it has one), and over the internet where the deal could be made online
- A website where deals can be made must carry a dedicated cancellation link on its homepage, placed prominently, with the cancellation methods next to it
- The consumer only has to state their name and ID number, plus, for a phone or oral notice, any additional identifying detail agreed when the deal was made
- The business must disclose the cancellation methods and the contact details for every channel in writing, no later than supply, and also on every invoice, receipt or payment notice and on the homepage, grouped together with special prominence (s.14ט(ה)-(ו))
- A continuing transaction ends within 3 business days of the notice, or 6 business days if sent by registered mail (s.13ד(ג))

## Void Clauses
The law applies despite any waiver or contrary agreement (s.36). As limits on the statutory distance-sale
right, do not write: "store credit
only", "sale items are final", "returns only with tags and in the original packaging", or "by
ordering you waive your rights". A voluntary policy that goes beyond the statute may set its own
conditions for the extra coverage only.

## Price Display Requirements

### Total Price (s.17א, s.17ד)
- The displayed price is the total price: all payments plus all taxes the business collects, including VAT (18%) and any accompanying charge the consumer cannot practically decline
- An exempt dealer (עוסק פטור) collects no VAT, so it shows no VAT line
- Prices are shown in Israeli currency only, except for the items in Part B of the First Schedule (s.17ז(ב))
- Display format: "₪X.XX" or "X.XX ש"ח", both are acceptable

### Delivery Costs
- An unavoidable delivery charge is part of the total price
- An optional delivery choice may be priced separately, but must be disclosed before the purchase

### Unit Price
The Consumer Protection (Price per Unit of Measure) Regulations 2008 apply to every distance sale:
for covered goods, show the price per unit of measure next to the product price.

### Discounts and Sales
- State the price before the special sale and the sale price; if the earlier price was in effect for less than 21 consecutive days, state the period it was in effect (1983 Regulations, reg. 6)
- State which goods the sale includes and its conditions (s.15)

### Card Payments
A business that accepts payment cards may not require a minimum purchase for card payment (s.12א,
Amendment 72, 2025).

## Required Business Disclosures
1. **Business name**, as registered
2. **ID or company number**
3. **Address** in Israel and abroad, not just a PO Box
4. **Contact details for every cancellation channel** (phone, email and so on, s.14ט(ד))

These details must appear on the website and in the written disclosure document.

## IS 5568 Accessibility Standard (Summary)
- Regulations 35 to 35ו of the Service Accessibility Regulations 2013 (as replaced in 2017) require web accessibility at Level AA under IS 5568
- IS 5568 Part 1 (2023) adopts WCAG 2.0 with Israeli national changes
- Exempt: an exempt dealer, or average annual turnover up to 100,000 NIS. Up to 1,000,000 NIS: exempt only for a site or app operating before the 2017 regulations, for 3 years at a time. A certified auditor may exempt a specific adjustment the platform cannot support, for up to 3 years. The 300,000 NIS exemption expired on 26 October 2020
- At least 25 employees: appoint an accessibility coordinator (reg. 91)
- Accessibility statement page required (reg. 35ה)
- A deviation is a breach only if not fixed within a reasonable time and no later than 60 days after a notice, with an alternative provided meanwhile; statutory damages can reach 50,000 NIS without proof of harm

## Cookie Consent Requirements
Israel has no cookie-specific statute (unlike the EU ePrivacy Directive). Consent rules flow from
the Privacy Protection Law, as amended by Amendment 13, and from the Privacy Protection Authority's
final opinion on consent (25.2.2026), which requires informed consent. Opt-in for advertising pixels
and non-essential cookies is prudent practice, not a statutory rule. Section 30A of the
Communications Law covers advertising messages, not cookies. Treat the following as best practice:
- Non-essential cookies should use explicit opt-in consent
- Essential cookies (session, authentication, cart) are exempt
- Consent banner should appear before non-essential cookies are set
- Users must be able to withdraw consent easily
- Cookie types and purposes must be clearly described
- Privacy policy must include cookie information
